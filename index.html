Background

Introduction to tennis

Tennis is a racket sport played in singles or doubles. Players hit a ball over the net into the opponent’s court, which requires agility, speed, coordination, strength, and endurance to respond to the ball, execute strokes, and recover for the next shot.

Physical demands of tennis

Play is made of repeated short bursts of high-intensity activity, with brief recovery between points. Players accelerate, decelerate, change direction, and keep their balance while coordinating footwork, racket movement, and whole-body actions (Pluim et al., 2023; Kovacs, 2007).

Speed & agility

Reaching the ball and changing direction quickly.

Strength & power

Producing force during strokes, serves, and explosive movements.

Coordination & balance

Synchronizing footwork, racket actions, and body positioning.

Endurance

Repeating high-intensity efforts and recovering quickly across a long match.

Key takeaway. Tennis performance depends on more than hitting the ball well. It requires the physical fitness to move efficiently, execute techniques effectively, and recover for the next point.

Key concepts in this program

  • Specificity: training should match the demands of tennis, which are multidirectional and intermittent (Pluim et al., 2023; Kovacs, 2007).
  • Progressive overload: demands increase gradually, from Preparation to Competition (coach-adapted).
  • Recovery: rest between demanding sessions, such as at least 48 hours between sprint sessions (Zhou et al., 2024).

The program / 01

Program overview

The Tennis Mesocycle Training Program develops the physical fitness components and movement skills tennis requires. It focuses on speed, agility, strength, power, coordination, balance, and muscular and cardiovascular endurance through tennis-specific exercises and progressive training.

The program runs in three phases: Preparation, Pre-competition, and Competition. Each phase has its own training focus, exercises, prescriptions, progression, recovery, and safety considerations. Research on the physical demands of tennis provides context for this design (Pluim et al., 2023; Kovacs, 2007).

Main goal

Improve tennis-specific physical fitness and movement performance through a structured, progressive program.

Target participants

Tennis players who want to develop their physical fitness and tennis-specific movement skills.

Training focus

Footwork, racket movement, body movement, physical conditioning, progression, recovery, and injury prevention.

Intended outcomes

These are intended outcomes, not guaranteed results. Actual improvement depends on the participant, training consistency, appropriate progression, and recovery.

Phase 1

Preparation phase

Foundation stage

FUNCTION(Goal of the phase)

To establish a foundation of physical fitness by developing movement control, strength, balance, and coordination, which will prepare the player for more demanding tennis-specific training in the next phases.

Key exercises

The exercises in this phase focus on footwork, racket movement, and body control to build a solid foundation for tennis performance.

Note on prescriptions. The volume, intensity, and rest values in this phase are suggested starting points. Adjust them with a qualified coach based on the participant’s fitness and readiness.

Movement analysis

Forehand racket speed depends on pelvis and trunk rotation and shoulder internal rotation (Reid et al., 2013; Elliott, 2006). In the two-handed backhand, trunk rotation increases racket handle velocity (Stępień et al., 2011). Contact point and racket-face orientation are coach-adapted cues.

Exercise 1

Multidirectional sprint training

Target areasLegs, hips, core

  1. 1Setup: Mark a 3 × 3 m grid with cones or buckets at the four corners and the centre (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Fixed-route drill (Weeks 1–2): Sprint from the centre to a corner, return to the centre, then go to the next marked point in a set pattern. Focus on a quick first step and controlled braking. (coach-adapted).
  3. 3Random-route drill (Weeks 2–3): A coach or partner calls a direction by voice, hand signal, or light. The athlete sprints to that cone and returns to the centre. (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Cones or buckets for a 3 × 3 m grid
  • A coach or partner to call directions (voice, hand signal, or light)

Volume

5 sets per session

  • Week 1: 3 routes per set
  • Week 2: 4 routes per set
  • Week 3: 5 routes per set
  • Weeks 4–6: 5 routes per set (coach-adapted continuation)

Rest more than 30 seconds between sprints and 5 minutes between sets. 3 sessions per week for 6 weeks (Weeks 4–6 coach-adapted).

Intensity

About 90% effort (coach-adapted)

The study used maximal effort in trained players.

Progression

  • Week 1: Fixed route, focusing on technique.
  • Week 2: Mix of fixed and random routes, adding sprints or sets.
  • Week 3: Mostly random route, with reaction calls as the main element (coach-adapted).
  • Weeks 4–6: keep random routes with reaction calls (coach-adapted).

References: Zhou et al., 2024.

Exercise 2

Shadow swing training

Target areasShoulders, trunk, hips

  1. 1Ready position: Stand with feet about shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and the racket held comfortably in front of the body (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Forehand: Rotate the pelvis and trunk (Reid et al., 2013).
  3. 3Shoulder: Bring the shoulder into internal rotation as the trunk rotates (Reid et al., 2013; Elliott, 2006).
  4. 4Two-handed backhand: Rotate the trunk through the whole stroke (Stępień et al., 2011).
  5. 5Serve: Rehearse the toss and racket motion without hitting a ball. Toss and racket kinematics of the professional serve are described in Reid, Reid, Whiteside & Elliott (2011); the rehearsal format is coach-adapted.
  6. 6Progress to hand-fed balls once technique is controlled (coach-adapted).

Technique checkpoints

  • Trunk rotation drives the forehand, so start the turn before the arm (Reid et al., 2013).
  • Hold a complete follow-through after each swing (coach-adapted).
  • Return to the ready position before the next swing (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Racket
  • Full-length mirror or clear wall space
  • Phone or camera for side-angle video (optional)
  • Court shoes

Volume

  • Weeks 1–2: 2 sets × 10 swings
  • Weeks 3–4: 2–3 sets × 12–15 swings
  • Weeks 5–6: 3 sets × 15 swings

1 min rest between sets (coach-adapted). 3 sessions per week for 6 weeks (coach-adapted).

Intensity

  • Weeks 1–2: easy, about RPE 3–4
  • Weeks 3–4: steady, about RPE 5–6
  • Weeks 5–6: match speed, about RPE 7–8

RPE mapping is coach-adapted (Borg, 1982).

Progression

  • Weeks 1–2: easy effort, technique checkpoints.
  • Weeks 3–4: steady effort, add a small step before each swing.
  • Weeks 5–6: match speed, footwork and balance controlled.

References: Reid et al., 2013; Elliott, 2006; Stępień et al., 2011; Reid, Whiteside & Elliott, 2011; Borg, 1982.

Exercise 3

Medicine ball rotational throw

Target areasTrunk, hips, shoulders

  1. 1Starting position: Stand with feet about shoulder-width apart, knees slightly bent, and hold the medicine ball with both hands in front of the body (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Prepare the rotation: Rotate the hips and trunk to one side and shift weight onto the back leg (coach-adapted).
  3. 3Throw side-on against a wall in a lateral position that simulates tennis (Roetert et al., 2009, as cited in Genevois et al., 2014).
  4. 4Use a one-hand lateral throw, as it correlates with forehand ball speed (Genevois et al., 2014).
  5. 5Control the finish: Keep balance after release, with knees slightly bent (coach-adapted).
  6. 6Progress to a 2–3 kg ball once technique is sound (Roetert &amp; Kovacs, 2019).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • 2–3 kg medicine ball
  • Sturdy wall or partner
  • Clear space

Volume

Proposed recommendation, not a verified protocol.
  • Weeks 1–2: 3 sets × 4 throws per side, light ball (about 2 kg)
  • Weeks 3–4: 3 sets × 4–5 throws per side, ball 2–3 kg
  • Weeks 5–6: 3–4 sets × 5 throws per side, add speed with controlled technique

Rest 60–90 s between sets (coach-adapted). 2 sessions per week for 6 weeks. Ball weights per week are coach-adapted.

Intensity

  • Weeks 1–2: light, about RPE 3–4
  • Weeks 3–4: light to moderate, about RPE 4–5
  • Weeks 5–6: moderate, about RPE 5–6, with controlled technique

RPE mapping is coach-adapted; RPE reliability is limited in resistance training.

Progression

  • Weeks 1–2: focus on technique with a light ball.
  • Weeks 3–4: move to a 2–3 kg ball as technique holds.
  • Weeks 5–6: increase throwing speed while keeping rotation and balance.

Safety

Keep a safe distance from the wall and catch the ball with control (coach-adapted).

References: Genevois et al., 2014; Genevois et al., 2013; Roetert et al., 2009, as cited in Genevois et al., 2014; Roetert & Kovacs, 2019.

Supporting exercise

Jump-and-stick landing drill

Target areasAnkles, knees, hips

  1. 1Stand on a flat, non-slip surface with feet about hip-width apart (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Jump a small height and land softly, with greater knee and hip flexion (coach-adapted).
  3. 3Hold the landing with control before resetting (coach-adapted).
  4. 4Reset upright before the next jump (coach-adapted).
  5. 5Progress to slightly higher jumps only when landings are consistently controlled (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Flat, non-slip surface
  • Optional floor markers for landing position

Volume

  • Weeks 1–2: 4 sets × 10 two-foot jumps (80 foot contacts)
  • Weeks 3–4: 4 sets × 10 two-foot jumps (80 foot contacts), focus on soft landings
  • Weeks 5–6: 4 sets × 10 jumps, slightly higher only when landings are controlled

Rest 1–2 min between sets (coach-adapted). 2–3 sessions per week on nonconsecutive days.

Intensity

  • Weeks 1–2: low, small vertical jumps
  • Weeks 3–4: low, soft and controlled landings
  • Weeks 5–6: low to moderate, only when landings are controlled

Progression

  • Weeks 1–2: small vertical jumps, technique focus.
  • Weeks 3–4: hold each landing with control.
  • Weeks 5–6: slightly higher jumps only when landings are controlled.

References: none (coach-adapted).

Safety

  • Use a dry, non-slip surface with adequate space (coach-adapted).

Phase 02 / Specificity

Pre-competition

Function. Turn the base into tennis-specific speed, power, and reaction. The player changes direction on cues and hits with control under time pressure.

Builds on Phase 1: sprints gain random cues, shadow swings gain ball contact, and rotational throws increase in weight and speed.

Exercise 1

Multidirectional sprint training, random cues

Target areasLegs, hips, core

  1. 1Setup: Use the same 3 × 3 m grid as Phase 1 (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Random-route drill: A coach or partner calls the direction with a hand signal or colored cone, and the athlete sprints to that marker and returns to the centre (Zhou et al., 2024).
  3. 3Finish each route with a tennis ball retrieval (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Cones
  • Tennis balls
  • A coach or cue system

Volume

  • Week 7: 3 sets × 4 routes
  • Week 8: 4 sets × 5 routes
  • Week 9: 4 sets × 5 routes

Rest 60–90 s between routes and 2–3 min between sets (coach-adapted). 2–3 sessions per week, one with a speed focus (coach-adapted). Weeks 7–9 extend Zhou et al. (2024), which used 3–5 routes over 3 weeks; the extension is coach-adapted.

Intensity

  • Week 7: RPE 7, controlled acceleration
  • Week 8: RPE 7–8, full acceleration
  • Week 9: RPE 8, full acceleration with controlled stops

RPE mapping is coach-adapted; RPE reliability is limited in resistance training.

Progression

  • Week 7: random cues at controlled speed.
  • Week 8: add full acceleration on random routes.
  • Week 9: reaction calls as the main element, at full acceleration.

Weekly progression is coach-adapted.

References: Zhou et al., 2024.

Exercise 2

Shadow swings to ball contact

Target areasShoulders, trunk, hips

  1. 1Start with shadow swings to set technique (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Hit balls fed by hand from forehand, backhand, and serve positions. Progress from slow to moderate speed (coach-adapted).
  3. 3Keep the swing path and follow-through from Phase 1 (coach-adapted).

Technique checkpoints

  • Trunk rotation drives the forehand, so start the turn before the arm (Reid et al., 2013).
  • Keep the racket path and follow-through consistent as balls are added (coach-adapted).
  • Return to the ready position between balls (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Racket
  • A basket of tennis balls
  • A court or open space

Volume

  • Week 7: 3 sets × 8 balls per stroke
  • Week 8: 3–4 sets × 10 balls per stroke
  • Week 9: 4 sets × 10 balls per stroke

Rest 30–60 s between sets (coach-adapted). 2–3 sessions per week (Smith, 2018, an opinion article, not a research study; stroke repetition, not ball-specific).

Intensity

  • Week 7: RPE 5–6, slow hand-fed balls
  • Week 8: RPE 6–7, moderate speed
  • Week 9: RPE 6–7, moderate speed across all strokes

RPE mapping is coach-adapted (Borg, 1982).

Progression

  • Week 7: hand-fed balls, forehand and backhand.
  • Week 8: add serve position and mixed strokes.
  • Week 9: consistent contact at moderate speed.

Weekly progression is coach-adapted.

Safety

  • Keep a safe distance from the feeding partner and other players (coach-adapted).
  • Keep swing speed controlled (coach-adapted).

References: Smith, 2018; Borg, 1982.

Exercise 3

Medicine ball rotational throw

Target areasTrunk, hips, shoulders

  1. 1Throw side-on against a sturdy wall, rotating the hips and trunk and shifting weight from the back leg to the front (Roetert et al., 2009, as cited in Genevois et al., 2014).
  2. 2Use a one-hand lateral throw, as it correlates with forehand ball speed (Genevois et al., 2014).
  3. 3Increase ball weight before adding speed, and only when rotation and balance stay controlled (Roetert &amp; Kovacs, 2019).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Medicine ball, starting light and moving up in weight
  • A sturdy wall or partner
  • Clear space

Volume

  • Week 7: 3 sets × 4 throws per side, about 2–3 kg
  • Week 8: 3 sets × 5 throws per side, about 3 kg
  • Week 9: 4 sets × 5 throws per side, heavier ball only when controlled (coach-adapted)

Rest 60–90 s between sets (coach-adapted). 6 weeks (Genevois et al., 2013); 2 sessions per week (coach-adapted). Ball weights above 2–3 kg are coach-adapted (Roetert &amp; Kovacs, 2019, for a rotational medicine ball exercise).

Intensity

  • Week 7: light to moderate, RPE 5–6
  • Week 8: moderate, RPE 6
  • Week 9: moderate, RPE 7, with controlled technique

RPE mapping is coach-adapted; RPE reliability is limited in resistance training.

Progression

  • Week 7: focus on technique with a 2–3 kg ball.
  • Week 8: move to about 3 kg as rotation and balance hold.
  • Week 9: add speed on the heavier ball only with controlled technique.

Weekly progression is coach-adapted.

Safety

  • Keep a safe distance from the wall and catch the ball with control (coach-adapted).

References: Genevois et al., 2014; Genevois et al., 2013; Roetert et al., 2009, as cited in Genevois et al., 2014; Roetert & Kovacs, 2019.

Supporting exercise

Jump-and-stick landing drill

Target areasAnkles, knees, hips

  1. 1Stand on a flat, non-slip surface with feet about hip-width apart (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Jump a small height and land softly, with greater knee and hip flexion (coach-adapted).
  3. 3Hold the landing with control before resetting (coach-adapted).
  4. 4Reset upright before the next jump (coach-adapted).
  5. 5Progress to slightly higher jumps only when landings are consistently controlled (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Flat, non-slip surface
  • Optional floor markers for landing position

Volume

  • Week 7: 4 sets × 10 two-foot jumps (80 foot contacts)
  • Week 8: 4 sets × 10 two-foot jumps (80 foot contacts), focus on soft landings
  • Week 9: 4 sets × 10 jumps, slightly higher only when landings are controlled

Rest 1–2 min between sets (coach-adapted). 2–3 sessions per week on nonconsecutive days (coach-adapted). Weeks 7–9 volume is coach-adapted; the 80-contact guidance is for novice adults (coach-adapted).

Intensity

  • Week 7: low, soft and controlled landings
  • Week 8: low, hold each landing with control
  • Week 9: low to moderate, only when landings are controlled

Weekly intensity is coach-adapted.

Progression

  • Week 7: soft landings, technique focus.
  • Week 8: hold each landing with control.
  • Week 9: slightly higher jumps only when landings are controlled.

Weekly progression is coach-adapted.

References: none (coach-adapted).

Phase 03 / Readiness

Competition

Function. Hold the speed, power, and technique built in Phases 1 and 2 while keeping the body fresh. Work is short and sharp, with full recovery between efforts. Starts at Week 10 and repeats weekly during the season (coach-adapted).

Builds on Phase 2: the same sprint and throw work, cut to about half the volume and paced for match-level bursts. The cut is coach-adapted, not from a cited study. Shadow swings become a warm-up.

Exercise 1

Multidirectional sprint training, match bursts

Target areasLegs, hips, core

  1. 1Setup: Use the same 3 × 3 m grid, with cones or buckets (coach-adapted).
  2. 2Random-cue bursts: A coach or partner signals the direction, and the athlete sprints to the marker and returns to the centre (Zhou et al., 2024).
  3. 3Keep the work short: 2–3 bursts per set, walking back to the start between bursts (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Cones or buckets
  • Tennis balls
  • A coach or cue system

Volume

  • Training week: 2 sets × 3–4 routes
  • Match week: 1 set × 3 routes

Rest 2–3 min between sets (coach-adapted). 1–2 sessions per week (coach-adapted), with fewer in congested match weeks (Fleming et al., 2023).

Intensity

  • Near-maximal bursts, about RPE 8 (coach-adapted)

The study used maximal effort in trained players (Zhou et al., 2024).

Progression

  • Reduce sprint volume in congested match weeks (Fleming et al., 2023).
  • Extra on-tour training may lower speed and aerobic capacity, so keep the load limited (Murphy et al., 2015).

References: Zhou et al., 2024; Fleming et al., 2023; Murphy et al., 2015.

Exercise 2

Shadow swings at match speed

Target areasShoulders, trunk, hips

  1. 1Warm up with 10 minutes of low running, stretching, and short submaximal sprints (Zhou et al., 2024).
  2. 2Shadow swings for forehand and backhand, focusing on swing path and follow-through (coach-adapted).
  3. 3Keep swings controlled, and stop before technique changes (coach-adapted).

Technique checkpoints

  • Trunk rotation drives the two-handed backhand (Stępień et al., 2011).
  • Keep the swing controlled and within a comfortable range at match speed (coach-adapted).
  • Stop before technique changes, rather than adding repetitions (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Racket
  • Open court space

Volume

  • Training week: 1–2 sets × 5–6 swings per stroke
  • Match week: 1 set × 5 swings per stroke

Rest 30–60 s between strokes (coach-adapted). Before each session or match (coach-adapted).

Intensity

  • About RPE 5–6 (coach-adapted)

RPE mapping is coach-adapted (Borg, 1982).

Progression

  • Keep shadow swings as a warm-up, not as added load (coach-adapted).
  • Avoid extra load in congested weeks (Fleming et al., 2023).

Safety

  • Keep swings controlled and within a comfortable range (coach-adapted).

References: Zhou et al., 2024; Fleming et al., 2023; Borg, 1982.

Exercise 3

Low-volume medicine ball throw

Target areasTrunk, hips, shoulders

  1. 1Throw side-on against a sturdy wall, rotating the hips and trunk (Roetert et al., 2009, as cited in Genevois et al., 2014).
  2. 2Keep the ball light, and stop before technique starts to change (coach-adapted).
Video demonstration (source: YouTube)

Equipment needed

  • Light medicine ball
  • A sturdy wall or partner
  • Clear space

Volume

  • Training week: 2 sets × 3 throws per side
  • Match week: 1 set × 3 throws per side

Rest 60–90 s between sets (coach-adapted). A maintenance program before a tournament helps prevent detraining and injury (coach-adapted).

Intensity

  • About RPE 5–6 (coach-adapted)

RPE mapping is coach-adapted (Borg, 1982).

Progression

  • Keep the ball weight unchanged during tournament weeks (coach-adapted).
  • Maintenance only, not a load increase (coach-adapted).

References: Genevois et al., 2014; Borg, 1982.

Basis

  • 48 hours between sprint sessions (Zhou et al., 2024).
  • Landing sessions 2–3 per week on nonconsecutive days (coach-adapted).
  • Shadow forehand training over 6 weeks (coach-adapted). The 3 sessions per week are coach-adapted, since the abstract does not state the frequency.
  • Stroke repetition 3–4 sessions per week (Smith, 2018).
  • Medicine ball 6 weeks (Genevois et al., 2013); 2 sessions per week (coach-adapted).
  • Reduced sprint and jump load in congested match weeks (Fleming et al., 2023; Murphy et al., 2015).
  • Maintenance before a tournament is coach-adapted.
  • Day placement and weekly load steps are coach-adapted.

Player wellbeing

Safety

Warm-up

  • 10 minutes before each session (Zhou et al., 2024).
  • Cool-down after each session (coach-adapted).

Recovery

  • At least 48 hours between sprint sessions (Zhou et al., 2024).
  • Rest on Saturday and Sunday (coach-adapted).

Stop rules

  • Stop if sharp pain or unusual symptoms occur (coach-adapted).
  • Repeat the phase for one more week if form breaks down (coach-adapted).

  • 10 minutes before each session (Zhou et al., 2024).

Stop rules

  • Stop a drill if control breaks down (coach-adapted).
  • Stop if pain occurs (coach-adapted).

Warm-up

  • 10 minutes before sprint sessions (Zhou et al., 2024).
  • Shadow swings as a warm-up after the general warm-up (coach-adapted).

Recovery

  • Full recovery between bursts (coach-adapted).
  • Reduce sprint and jump load in congested match weeks (Fleming et al., 2023; Murphy et al., 2015).

Stop rules

  • Check fatigue, soreness, and pain before each session (Fleming et al., 2023).
  • Reduce or stop bursts if performance drops (Fleming et al., 2023; coach-adapted).
  • Stop if pain occurs (coach-adapted).
